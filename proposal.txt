CAT Autonomy Challenge: Navigation Solution Proposal

Caterpillar Autonomy Challenge: Navigation
and Autonomy Solution Proposal
For the MaRS Autonavigation team discussion. Stack: ROS 2 Humble, Nav2 and RTAB-Map
SLAM running together, a ZED 2i stereo camera plus IMU and wheel encoders, ArUco
markers as the only absolute reference (detector and marker pose by the imageprocessing team), and a claw that drops from underneath the rover to pick up sand. This
is revision 3 (updated for a skid-steer base), simplified after review: custom nodes that
RTAB-Map or the behavior tree already cover were removed. Numbers marked
assumption must be measured on the real robot.

Proposed solution at a glance
What we will build. A four-wheel skid-steer rover (ros2_control differential drive) that
runs RTAB-Map SLAM and Nav2 together on a ZED 2i. Tuned ZED visual-inertial odometry,
fused with wheel speed and the ZED gyro in ekf_local , carries the pose across the
arena. ArUco markers near the start corner correct the map at the start and again on the
return. Boulders come from obstacles_detection , Nav2 (Smac Lattice planner,
Regulated Pure Pursuit, edited behavior tree) drives, and a small Python mission tree runs
the loop: find markers, travel, claw, return, release, repeat. Only four pieces are custom;
everything else is existing software.
How a run goes.
1. Setup (10 minutes): place the tags on the frame, start all nodes with a fresh RTAB-Map
database, check the ZED and the wheel odometry.
2. Lock: the operator may drive the rover by remote control inside the start zone only, to a
clear spot and a heading where markers are visible; RTAB-Map records them. Then the
team declares hands-free to the judge.
3. Anchor: the arena anchor publishes map -> arena from marker 0, and the zones are
loaded from one YAML file.
4. Travel (250 points): the mission tree sends Nav2 to the excavation staging pose; Nav2
plans a slalom around boulders and craters it sees.
5. Excavate (75 points): inside the excavation zone only, the claw drops, grabs sand and
lifts.
6. Return: the mission tree navigates to the construction zone, markers come back into
view and RTAB-Map corrects the pose, then a short move to the dump pose.

8. Repeat while time remains (full autonomy, 450 or 600 points). If a step keeps failing,

1. Executive summary (revised)
We run RTAB-Map SLAM and Nav2 together, live, on a ZED 2i, and build only four small
custom pieces; everything else is existing software configured for our rover.
What changed since the first draft

The camera is a ZED 2i stereo camera, and the claw drops from underneath the rover,
so the excavation task is one action (drop, grab, lift) and nothing needs to be filtered
out of the camera view.
RTAB-Map runs in SLAM mode at the same time as Nav2, because the arena cannot be
mapped beforehand. It builds the map as the rover moves and uses the ArUco markers
as landmarks to correct drift.
Odometry is tuned, not replaced: ZED visual-inertial tracking plus wheel odometry in
the existing ekf_local . Per your senior, tuned odometry should keep drift small.
Nodes that duplicated RTAB-Map or the behavior tree were removed (pose lock
manager, marker localizer, global EKF, zone monitor and others). Section 6 lists what
stays and why.
New section 7 explains the default Nav2 behavior tree and exactly how we edit it.
What changed in revision 3 (skid-steer base). The planner is now Smac Lattice with
differential primitives instead of Hybrid-A-star; wheel odometry feeds only forward speed
into ekf_local because skid-steer wheel yaw is unreliable; recoveries are retuned for
pivoting on sand; the base interface is a standard diff-drive controller rather than a
custom node; and section 12 records every option chosen or rejected, with reasons.
The problem in one line. Starting from a random pose and orientation, the rover must
cross a 9 m x 5 m sand arena with random boulders and craters (touching or entering
either costs points), pick up sand only in the far-right excavation zone with the claw, carry
it to a 1.25 m x 0.9 m berm target, release it, and ideally repeat, all hands-free. GPS,
compass, ultrasonic and touch sensors are banned, and the only navigation references
are up to 5 ArUco markers around the start zone.
The solution.
1. Odometry: ZED visual-inertial tracking, tuned, fused with wheel odometry and IMU in
ekf_local (2D mode). This is the main drift control.

2. Map and localization: RTAB-Map in SLAM mode, running live next to Nav2. It publishes
/rtabmap/map and map -> odom , and the ArUco markers go in as landmarks so the

map is corrected whenever markers are seen (at the start and on return).
3. Obstacles: the obstacles_detection pipeline you already have for boulders; test it on
real craters before writing anything custom.
4. Navigation: Nav2 with a footprint-aware planner and Regulated Pure Pursuit, using a
lightly edited copy of the default Nav2 behavior tree (section 7).

5. Mission: a small Python behavior tree ( py_trees_ros ) that rotates to see the markers,
travels, triggers the claw, returns, and releases the sand, with retries and timeouts.
6. Custom code: only four pieces (a tag adapter and arena anchor, the mission tree, the
claw interface from the mechanical team, and a crater detector only if testing shows
we need it).
What you need from other teams: marker detections with pose (image team), Excavate
and Dump actions (mechanical team), a base that accepts cmd_vel and reports wheel
odometry (controls).
Biggest risks: drift on loose sand; craters that the stereo depth does not reveal; marker
visibility at range; the rule against using walls; and the existing rtabmap.launch.py bug,
which now matters directly because mapping mode is the mode we will use. Section 10
has mitigations and section 11 the decisions to settle.

2.3 Arena geometry and coordinate frame (from the diagram; verify on site)
Total 9 m (x, long axis) by 5 m (y, short axis).
Start zone: top left, about 2 m wide and about 3.5 m tall (5 m minus the 1.5 m
construction zone). No rocks or craters inside.
Construction zone: bottom left, 2 m x 1.5 m, containing the target berm area of 1.25 m x
0.9 m.
Obstacle zone: middle, 5.5 m wide.
Excavation zone: right, 1.5 m wide, 5 m tall.
Proposed arena frame: origin at the bottom-left inner corner of the arena, x toward the
excavation zone, y toward the start zone. Then: construction zone x 0-2, y 0-1.5; start zone
x 0-2, y 1.5-5; obstacle zone x 2-7.5; excavation zone x 7.5-9. All zone polygons live in one
YAML file in this frame. RTAB-Map's own map frame starts at the rover's starting pose, so
the arena anchor (section 5.3) publishes map -> arena from marker 0. Marker positions
are measured against the arena frame during the 10 minute setup, using a tape measure
and a template jig.

7.5 to 9 m. So the right mental model is:

A 6x6 ArUco tag has 8 modules across (including its border), and you want about 5-6
pixels per module, so about 40-50 px across the tag. With the wide 2.1 mm lens that means
tags are usable to about 4 m at 1080p (5-6 m at 2K); with the 4 mm lens to about 8 m. A
trained detector can reach somewhat further, but pose quality drops with range. Ideal
range error is about Z x (0.5 px / tag pixels): roughly 1 cm at 3 m and 4 cm at 5 m with the 4
mm lens. Real-world error is typically 2-4 times larger because of blur, vibration, glare and
calibration, so plan to trust marker position fixes only within about 4-6 m. We only need
markers near the start zone and the construction corner (about 1.5-4 m away at the lock
and at the dump), so even the wide lens works, but the 4 mm lens gives a safer margin.
Implications for the image team: print the tags at the full 25 cm, matte and non-glare; pick
the dictionary with the biggest modules that still gives enough distinct ids (a 4x4
dictionary has larger modules than 6x6); and publish a confidence and reprojection error
with every pose. One rule point for us: the ZED 2i has a built-in magnetometer and
barometer, and the handbook bans compasses. Make sure nothing in the ZED tracking or
RTAB-Map configuration uses magnetometer data, and be ready to show the judges how
it is switched off.

What to tune (your senior's plan). Odometry quality is the main lever, so treat it as a
tuning task with numbers:
ZED tracking: IMU fusion on, 2D mode on if the wrapper offers it, sensible depth range
and confidence threshold. Consider turning off the ZED's own area memory so its
odometry stays free of jumps while RTAB-Map does the global correction (check the
exact option names in your wrapper version).
Wheel odometry: on a skid-steer base the wheels slip sideways in every turn, so wheel
yaw is unreliable: fuse only the wheels' forward speed, keep their yaw rate out of the
filter (or give it a very large covariance), and let the IMU gyro and the ZED supply yaw.
Calibrate wheel radius and the effective track width (larger than the geometric one)
with in-place turn tests on sand, and give wheel odometry a larger covariance than the
ZED in ekf_local .
How to measure: drive a marked 8 m straight line and a 90 degree turn ten times on
sand-like ground, log the end pose against a tape measure, and tune until the error
spread is acceptable.

5.3 Localization: RTAB-Map SLAM live, markers as landmarks
At the start the behavior tree rotates the rover slowly in place until a marker with a
good pose is seen and RTAB-Map has recorded it (a condition node watches the
detections). No separate lock node is needed. RTAB-Map's map frame begins at the
rover's starting pose.
Tying the map to the arena. Marker 0's position in the arena is measured during the 10
minute setup, so the arena frame is marker 0's frame plus a fixed offset. A small script
(the arena anchor) reads marker 0's pose in the map frame (from RTAB-Map's
landmark output if your version exposes it, otherwise from the detection plus TF) and
publishes map -> arena . Zones, staging poses and the arena boundary are defined in
the arena frame in one YAML file.
Crossing the obstacle zone: ekf_local carries the pose; RTAB-Map keeps adding
map nodes and the occupancy grid grows. Tuned odometry keeps the drift small
(section 3.2).
On the return the markers come back into view; RTAB-Map adds a landmark
constraint, optimises its graph and shifts map -> odom , which corrects the
accumulated drift before the dump. Nav2's global costmap simply rebuilds from the
updated /rtabmap/map .
Configuration: SLAM mode with a fresh database each run ( --delete_db_on_start ),
odometry from /odometry/local , visual_odometry:=false as you run it today, the
landmark input as documented for your rtabmap_slam version, and a modest
detection rate to save CPU. RTAB-Map can also detect ArUco tags itself through its
Marker parameters (verify the names in your version), which is a useful fallback until

the image team's detector is ready.

5.4 Perception
Boulders: keep obstacles_detection , retuned for sand. Start with a lower
Grid/MaxGroundAngle (about 15-20 degrees, an assumption to test), keep the range at
about 4-5 m (stereo depth error grows quickly with distance), and set ZED depth mode
and confidence threshold for sand. Passive stereo works in sunlight and on textured
sand, but check smooth, shadowed or very fine sand.
Craters: test before building. Put a real crater mock-up in front of the rover and watch
/obstacles and the 2D map. A steep crater wall is marked as an obstacle, but a

shallow crater below the slope limit looks like ground, and a hidden floor appears as
missing data. Geometry reminder: with the camera 0.7 m high and a crater 3 m ahead,
the grazing angle is only about 13 degrees, so the far rim of a 50 cm wide, 15 cm deep
crater hides the floor. Cheap fixes first: set Nav2 allow_unknown: false so the
planner will not drive through cells it never saw, and mount the camera higher (a
deployable mast gives a steeper view and is allowed after the start). Write a custom
crater detector only if these fail.
Walls: the handbook forbids using walls for autonomy, so we need an answer from the
organizers on whether wall returns in the costmap are acceptable. If not, the arena
anchor can also crop points outside the arena polygon and publish a virtual boundary
(a short cloud along the arena edge, built from known dimensions) so the planner stays
inside without sensing the walls.
Claw: it hangs underneath, so it is out of the camera's view and needs no self-filter.

7. Behavior trees: the default Nav2 tree and how we edit it (revised)
7.1 How a behavior tree runs
A behavior tree is ticked over and over (Nav2's bt_loop_duration is 10 ms in your
params). Each tick travels down from the root. Every node answers SUCCESS, FAILURE or
RUNNING. Control nodes decide which child gets the tick (Sequence, Fallback,
RecoveryNode). Action nodes call a server (plan a path, follow a path, spin) and stay
RUNNING until it answers. Condition nodes check something and answer at once (goal
updated?). A shared blackboard passes data such as {goal} and {path} between
nodes.

How it works, in plain words: plan once a second, drive along the path, and if stuck clear
the costmaps, then try spin, wait and back up in turn, giving up after six rounds.
1. The outer RecoveryNode runs its first child (the navigation). If that fails, it runs its
second child (the recovery fallback) and then tries the navigation again. After 6 failed
rounds it returns FAILURE and the NavigateToPose action aborts.
2. The first child is a PipelineSequence : both parts keep being ticked every cycle, so the
robot keeps following the path while the path is recomputed.
3. The RateController limits ComputePathToPose to once per second (1 Hz). If planning
fails, the inner RecoveryNode clears the global costmap and retries once, unless a new
goal arrived ( GoalUpdated ).

4. FollowPath hands the path to the controller server. If the controller fails (no progress,
collision ahead), its inner RecoveryNode clears the local costmap and retries once.
5. If it still fails, the RoundRobin in the recovery fallback picks one recovery per failure, in
order: clear both costmaps, then Spin 1.57 rad, then Wait 5 s, then BackUp 0.30 m, then
back to the first.
6. When the goal checker is satisfied, FollowPath returns SUCCESS and the whole tree
returns SUCCESS.

12.2 Caveats to test early and open points
Pivots inside a path. Regulated Pure Pursuit chases a look-ahead point, so it handles
the first rotation and arcs but not pivots in the middle of a path. Keep the lattice
rotation penalty high and test in simulation first; if it misbehaves, switch to MPPI with
the DiffDrive model.
Start zone clearance. The rover's circumscribed radius is about 0.9 m, so a full pivot
needs a circle about 1.8 m across, close to the 2 m start zone width read from the
diagram. The rulebook gives only the diagram value, so we design around it: use the
allowed remote-control step in the start zone to move the rover to a clear spot and a
heading that sees the markers before going hands-free, and treat autonomous pivots
near the frame as a fallback.
Lattice primitives. Confirm the sample differential primitives exist in your Humble
install, and decide the minimum turning radius after sand tests.
Base details. Four wheels, differential drive through ros2_control and one encoder per
side are confirmed. Wheelbase and track width are not measured yet; they are needed
later for the footprint and the track-width multiplier, but not for the proposal.
Simulation. The MaRs-AutoNav rover is swerve; the competition simulation needs a
skid-steer model, and its holonomic settings do not carry over.
