Welcome to the Caterpillar Autonomy Challenge!
The mission of your robot system is to provide a berm that protects astronauts and habitat modules
from plume ejecta from vehicles lifting off and landing on the opposite side of the berm to be
constructed. For this competition, plume ejecta is presumed to move perpendicular to the surface
and parallel to the short axis of the berm area. Both mass (or volume) of the berm and the projected
area of the berm normal to the plume ejecta provide this protection.

ARENA SPECIFICATIONS AND PROTOCOL
Specifications:
1. The arena measures approximately 9 m × 5 m and contains ~30 cm depth of tilled, dry sand.
2. Obstacle Zone - The judges will attempt to construct the obstacle field in such a way as to require obstacle
detection, mapping, and navigation planning to determine a “slalom” route to reach the excavation zone.
3. At least three randomly placed boulder obstacles will be present in the obstacle zone before each run.
Boulders are approximately 30–40 cm in diameter with varying heights. Similar or smaller boulders may also
be present in the excavation zone.
4. At least three craters of varying depth and up to 40–50 cm width will be present in the obstacle zone.
5. Beacon and marker mounting requirements for navigation are provided in the final section of this document.
6. The arena diagram is strictly for reference. The exact location of rocks and craters may not be the same during
the actual competition runs.

Arena Protocol:
1. Once competition runs begin, the Arena becomes a restricted operational area.
2. Students must promptly follow all instructions from the Arena Chief and Arena personnel. Faculty/advisors
are prohibited from entering the Arena during competition.
3. No entry into the Arena is permitted during a run except in emergencies.
4. Finalist teams will be assigned booths for setup and final modifications.

5. Teams may enter the Moon Yard Arena staging area only when authorized by the Arena Chief. Approaching the
Arena before authorization is prohibited.
6. For Arena operations, the active team is strictly limited to a maximum of 4 members for robot setup/removal
and a maximum of 4 members for Mission Control.
7. Teams will receive a Pre-Task Briefing (PTB) with run-specific information before entering the Arena.
8. When directed, teams must place their robot in the Arena and establish communication with the MCC. Robots
will start from a randomly assigned position and orientation. Positive and negative obstacles (e.g., boulders
and craters) must be avoided and are not traversable.
9. Team members shall remain with their robot in the designated Arena staging area until directed otherwise by
the Arena Chief or designated representative.
10. Teams must promptly exit the Arena when setup is complete or when instructed by Arena personnel.
11. At the end of the run, teams must promptly retrieve all robots and equipment from the Arena.
12. Student cell phones, cameras, tablets, and other restricted electronics devices brought into the Moon Yard
Arena shall be placed on the Arena Chief’s station to be retrieved later. No exceptions.

ROBOTS AND ROBOTIC OPERATIONS
Robots:
1. Berm construction requires teams to consider several design and operation factors such as efficient
communications, minimizing vehicle mass, minimizing energy/power required, and maximizing autonomy.
2. Components (i.e. electronic, mechanical, etc.) are not required to be space qualified for atmospheric,
electromagnetic, thermal, or Lunar environments.

Robot Requirements:
1. Volumetric dimensions — Robot(s) shall be contained within a payload envelope measuring 1.5 m length ×
0.75 m width × 0.75 m height. The orientation of these dimensions may be chosen by the team.
2. The robot may deploy or expand beyond the envelope after the start of the attempt but shall not exceed 1.75
m in additional height (vertically). Multiple robot systems are allowed, but the starting dimensions of the
whole system (all robots combined) shall comply with the volumetric dimensions given in this rule.
3. Robots will be inspected for volumetric dimensions in the stowed configuration during the Safety Inspection.
No modifications or team interaction with the robot is permitted during this verification. Teams may make
modifications after inspection, but the robot must be re-inspected before approval for participation.
a. Robot Mass — Maximum mass: 80 kg. Subsystems/equipment on the robot used to transmit
commands/data and video to telerobotic operators are counted toward the mass limit. The mass of the
navigational aid system, including any beacons or targets not attached to the robot, is included in the
maximum robot mass limit and must be self-powered.
b. Equipment not on the robot used to receive data from and send commands to the robot for telerobotic
operations is excluded from the mass limit. Multiple robot systems are allowed, but the total mass of the
whole system shall comply with the mass limit given in this rule.
4. External robot antennas are recommended to reduce potential interference problems.
5. The robot may intentionally separate itself if desired, but all parts of the robot must remain under the team’s
control at all times. Unintentional breakage will not be counted against the team. The robot does not need to
reassemble before the end of the competition run.
6. The robot may operate either by telerobotic control (LOS or NLOS) or autonomously and cannot use touch
sensors to sense or avoid obstacles.
7. Teams are required to confirm the forward position of the robot, as the facing direction at the start of the run
will be decided on the spot by the judges.
8. Teams may use honeycomb structures provided they are strong enough to be safe and the edges are sealed to
prevent dust intrusion. Wheels with large honeycomb structures open on both sides are allowed as long as the
edges are not sharp enough to create a cutting hazard.
9. Teams may not use GPS, rubber pneumatic tires, ultrasonic proximity sensors, or hydraulics. Use of these will
result in failure during inspection.

10. Closed pneumatic systems are allowed if the gas is supplied entirely by the robot itself and remains selfcontained.

E-STOP Button:
1. Also known formally as an emergency brake, emergency stop (E-STOP), emergency off (EMO), kill switch, or
emergency power off (EPO), it is a safety mechanism used to shut off machinery in an emergency when it
cannot be shut down in the usual manner. The E-STOP function shall override all other robot controls and
commands when actuated.
2. The E-STOP should be unobstructed, with no collars or actuation restrictions, and easily accessible without
reaching over, under, or around components to actuate it (Indian machinery safety standards, IS 16818:2018
(Safety of Machinery — Emergency Stop Function, identical to ISO 13850:2015) and IS/IEC 60947‑5‑5:2016)
3. A second deliberate action shall be required to reset the E-STOP, such as pressing a reset button, twisting the
mushroom button until it springs back up, or pulling the button back up. It must not reset automatically.
4. The robot shall be equipped with an E-STOP button. An unmodified “Commercial Off-The-Shelf” (COTS) red
button is required. Teams must use sound engineering practices while placing and securing the E-STOP button
on the robot(s); failure to do so may result in safety disqualification. The E-STOP button shall have a minimum
diameter of 40 mm and require no additional steps to access.
5. Disabling the E-STOP button without authorization from the Staff shall result in safety disqualification.
6. There shall be only one E-STOP button per robot and, in the case of multiple robots, each robot shall have its
own E-STOP button.
7. The E-STOP button shall stop the construction robot’s motion and disable power with a single push motion. It
shall operate reliably and instantaneously. A closed control signal to a mechanical relay is allowed, provided it
remains open to disable the robot. (Compliance with these requirements is reinforced under the Machinery
and Electrical Equipment Safety (Omnibus Technical Regulation) Order, 2024, issued by the Government of
India.)The button shall disconnect the batteries from all controllers (high-current forklift-type button) and
isolate the batteries from the rest of the active subsystems.
8. Only onboard laptop computers may remain powered on if powered by their own independent internal
battery. For example, a small onboard battery powering only a Raspberry Pi control computer is acceptable,
provided its power does not flow through the main robot E-STOP button.

Power Meters / Data Loggers:
1. The robot shall provide its own onboard power. No external (facility) power will be provided during the
competition attempt. There are no specific power limitations; however, the robot must be entirely selfpowered, and all power sources must be included within the maximum allowable mass.
2. Any monitoring or logging devices must be schematically placed between the battery and the kill switch to
ensure that data is preserved even if the E-STOP button is activated. A 30-point reduction to Berm
Construction Productivity – Normalized for Energy (BCP Energy) will be applied if the robot is not wired in
accordance with this requirement. This will be checked during Inspection. Teams also risk scoring zero if the
E-STOP must be triggered while the robot is improperly wired.
3. These devices must also be mounted at the highest practical location on the robot and remain clearly visible
for inspection and monitoring purposes.
4. Energy consumption shall be recorded using a “Commercial Off-The-Shelf” (COTS) electronic data logger
device. Actual energy consumed during the attempt shall be shown to the judges on the data logger
immediately after the attempt (“immediate” includes the time required for the judge to enter the arena, locate
the logger, and record the reading).
5. If the logger is independently powered, the robot may be remotely powered off after the run.
6. Although acceptable, this is not recommended in situations where the robot may need to complete an
operation before removal from the arena.

Battery Protocol:

1. Battery containers must be designed for the safe storage, charging, and transportation of lithium-ion batteries
or approved equivalents (applicable to the Lithium-Ion / Ni-Cad batteries used in the robots).
2. Batteries must be stored in upright containers and must not come into contact with each other. Batteries that
have been dropped must be inspected for damage and replaced if necessary. Batteries that are hot to the touch
after charging must not be stored.
3. To ensure the robot is suitable for an actual mission, it must not employ any fundamental physical processes,
gases, fluids, or consumables that would not function in an off-world environment. For example, dust removal
from a lens or sensor must use a physical process suitable for the lunar surface.
4. Teams may use processes requiring an Earth-like environment (e.g., oxygen or water) only if the system using
those processes is designed to function in a lunar environment and if all such resources are included within
the robot’s mass.
5. The rules are intended to encourage robots that demonstrate plausible off-world system functionality;
however, components do not need to be traceable to off-world-qualified versions. Examples of allowable
components include Sealed Lead-Acid (SLA) or Nickel Metal Hydride (NiMH) batteries, composite materials,
rubber or plastic parts, actively fan-cooled electronics, brushed motors, infrared sensors, inertial
measurement units, and Hall Effect sensors. Teams may use these at their own risk.
6. If a battery continues to feel hot after charging, if possible, remove from the building and place outside and
notify a member of event staff as a nonemergency issue.
7. Teams shall immediately notify designated safety personnel of any fire, smoke, battery malfunction, or other
condition presenting an immediate safety hazard.

Robotic Operations:
1. The robot cannot be anchored to the sand prior to the beginning of the demonstration.
2. At the start of the competition run, the mining robot may not occupy any location outside the defined starting
position in the arena.
3. The mining robot may not penetrate the sand surface with more force than the weight of the robot before the
start of the competition attempt.
4. No ordnance, projectiles, far-reaching mechanisms, or similar systems may be used. The mining robot must
move on the sand surface.
5. In this context, a far-reaching mechanism does not include deployed or extended components allowed under
the dimensions rule, provided they do not violate this rule.
6. The robot must operate within the Arena; it is not permitted to pass beyond the confines of the outside
perimeter of the arena or hit the walls during the competition run. In the event of the robot ramming the
walls, a point penalty 50pts will be levied for twice. The third time it happens, the run will be cancelled.
7. If a robot separates, wall ram penalties apply collectively. The entire system (all parts combined) shares the
maximum limit of two allowed wall rams before run cancellation.
8. The robot may not use any process that changes the physical or chemical properties of the sand or otherwise
affects uniformity for the competition attempts. The mining robot may not penetrate the sand surface with
more force than its own weight.

Navigation Protocol:
1. Teams must declare their robot's orientation to the inspection judge using three clearly marked, nonconflicting arrows (permanent marker is acceptable) to indicate length (Arrow 1), width (Arrow 2), and a
designated "forward" starting direction (Arrow 3). Arrow 3 can be placed anywhere and point in any direction
(except straight up or down) and does not dictate the robot's actual driving path; it serves solely as a reference
point for judges to align the robot (North, South, East, or West) according to the pre-run random direction
spin.
2. Compasses (analog, digital), Global Positioning Satellite (GPS) or IMU-enabled GPS devices are not allowed.
Teams must explain to the judges how the device will be switched off or the data will be subtracted and ensure
the internal calculations, do not make use of the GPS or IMU-enabled GPS device.
3. The mass of the navigational aid system is included in the maximum construction robot mass limit of 80.0 kg
and must be self-powered.
4. Inertial measurement units (IMU) are allowed on the construction robot. Teams have to explain to the judges
how the compass feature will be switched off, or the compass data is subtracted to ensure the internal
calculations do not make use of the compass (from any magnetic field surrounding the robot).
5. During the competition attempt, the construction robot is limited to autonomous and telerobotic operations
only. LINE-OF-SIGHT OPERATION IS NOT ALLOWED.

Autonomy Rules:
1. Telemetry to monitor the health of the construction robot is allowed during the autonomous period. Teams
will need to remain “hands free” during their attempts at autonomy points. Teams shall explain to the
inspection/ attending judge before each competition run how they are interacting with the telemetry system,
and the judge will observe to ensure compliance with all competition rules.
2. Teams shall not touch the controls during the autonomous period. Orientation data cannot be transmitted to
the construction robot in the autonomous period. See complete details in the MCC and Autonomous
Operations.
3. The walls shall not be used for the purposes of mapping autonomous navigation and collision avoidance
(there are no walls on off-world locations). Touching or having a switch sensor spring wire that may brush on
a wall or any other surface as a collision avoidance sensor is not allowed (this includes touch sensors). Teams
shall not use the walls of the construction arena for sensing by the robot to achieve autonomy.
4. The team must explain to the inspection judges how their autonomous systems work and prove that the
autonomy sensors do not use the walls (there are no walls in off-world locations, and teams shall operate as
closely as possible in that scenario of operations). Integrity is expected of all team members and their faculty
advisors.
5. Teams are allowed to interact with an interface that allows different pieces of telemetry data to be viewed as
long as there is no real-time or other interaction to control or influence the robot.
6. Teams are not permitted to update or alter the autonomy program to account/detect or upload information
about obstacle locations

7. Failure to divulge the method of autonomy sensing shall result in disqualification from the competition.

Full Autonomy:
1. The robot must complete one full cycle in hands-free control.
2. The attempt must begin at the start of the run.
3. Teams may use remote control only within the starting zone for localization. The robot must then operate
autonomously through traversal, excavation, return, construction-zone entry, and sand dumping. A discernible
amount of sand must be deposited at the berm location, as determined by the MCJ.
4. Contact with a rock or crossing a crater in the obstacle zone incurs a −30 point penalty per occurrence, capped
at −90 points. No penalties apply in the excavation zone.
5. Successful completion requires full autonomous operation across all competition tasks.
6. Completing one full cycle as described awards 450 points, after which the team may return to remote control.
To earn the full 600 points, the robot must complete at least two full cycles, remain in hands-free control for
the entire competition run, and successfully achieve berm construction points as determined by the
volumetric scan.

MCC AND AUTONOMOUS OPERATIONS
MCC:
Teams will control or autonomously operate their robot from the MCC to simulate operations of a Lunar In-Situ
Resource Utilization (ISRU) construction mission. A Mission Control Director (MCD) will serve as the Lead Judge for
the MCC to maintain the integrity of MCC rules outlined in the guidebook and ensure consistent interpretation and
enforcement for all teams.

General guidelines:
1. A maximum of 4 team members may enter the MCC, and all must enter together when authorized.
2. Faculty/advisors are prohibited from entering the MCC.
3. Teams must bring all required mission-critical components and spares before entering. Forgotten items may
not be retrieved later.
4. Only devices required for robot operations are permitted in the MCC. Extra laptops, phones, smart devices,
etc., are prohibited.
5. No external communication is allowed after entering the MCC, except with Arena teammates during setup
using approved equipment.

6. All rule clarifications must be resolved before entering the MCC. The schedule will not be delayed for late
requests.
7. Judges are observers only and will not provide operational assistance or answer mid-run scoring/position
questions.
8. MCC and Arena judges may terminate setup or runs for rule violations.
9. Teams must conduct themselves professionally, as in a real-time space operation.
10. Teams must operate robots using sound engineering practices.
11. Rule violations, unprofessional conduct, unsportsmanlike behavior, or failure to follow staff directions may
result in penalties or disqualification.
12. Team members must comply with all directions from the MCJ. Disputes with MCJ direction or decisions must
be elevated through the MCD.

Mission Setup:
1. Teams may not connect or interact with any equipment in the MCC until the setup period begins.
2. Teams must indicate competition readiness to the judges as soon as their robot is ready.
3. During the setup period, the team members will be allowed to talk with the team members in the Arena over a
phone call. Once the setup time is over, all the phones are to be left outside the MCC before the competition
run begins.
4. Arena team members are prohibited from pointing out obstacles, craters, and other arena conditions to the
MCC team members.
5. Teams may use the situational awareness cameras during the setup period without penalty.

Mission Operations:
1. Verbal Autonomy Declaration: Before the robot can switch into "hands-free" autonomous mode, you must
now loudly and explicitly declare it to the Mission Control Judge (MCJ). Starting without a verbal declaration
disqualifies your points for that cycle.
Dump Autonomy Boundary: Control must be handed over to the computer before entering the Construction
Zone. If you cross into the zone under manual control, you are operationally banned from earning Dump
Autonomy points.
2. All autonomy attempts must be “Hands-Free”, meaning no team members are permitted to touch any
components (e.g., laptops, game controllers) brought into the MCC until the team has declared autonomy
completion or autonomy failure.
3. If a team member interacts with any equipment while the robot is still moving or before the team has declared
the autonomy attempt complete, the team will receive zero points for their attempt.
4. In the event of an autonomy failure, the team shall announce that the attempt has failed before resuming
manual control.
5. It is the sole responsibility of team members in the MCC to communicate effectively with the judges to ensure
every autonomy attempt is recognized and scored correctly. If the judge Is not notified of the attempt in
advance of the team initiating its execution, the score will be zero points.
6. Teams may select a maximum of two out of the three available situational awareness cameras (with penalties).
During autonomy attempts, these selected cameras may be manipulated, but adjustments are strictly limited
to a fixed set of pre-defined angles.
7. Telerobotic operators are only allowed to use data and video originating from the robot and the competition
video monitors.

Mission Anomalies:
1. Once the competition timer has started, the robot has 5 minutes to move on the mission. If the robot has not
moved by the 5-minute mark, the robot is considered inoperable, and the run will end.
2. As responsible engineers, the team should notify the judges that they are ending the run if their robot
experiences an unrecoverable issue that renders it incapable of executing key mission tasks. Such failures

Mission Conclusion:
1. All robot operations must stop when the competition timer expires. Ongoing autonomous actions must be
inhibited immediately, except active sand dumping already in progress may be completed.
2. Teams may not disconnect communications or dismantle MCC equipment until authorized by judges, as the
robot may still need to relocate or unload sand.
3. Teams must remain in the MCC until dismissed by the judges.
4. Teams are responsible for removing all equipment brought into the MCC. Forgotten items may not be retrieved
once the next team’s run has begun.

Communication System Requirements:
1. Use a standard IEEE 802.11 dual-band router supporting both 2.4 GHz and 5 GHz operation. The 2.4 GHz band
must be capable of being disabled.
2. Use the competition-assigned SSID provided during check-in for all wireless equipment.
3. Broadcast the assigned SSID; hidden networks are not permitted.
4. Enable wireless encryption (WPA2 or WPA3).
5. Transmit only on the assigned SSID.
6. Set the 2.4 GHz channel bandwidth to 20 MHz only (channel bonding is prohibited).
7. Ensure all wireless equipment complies with WPC regulations and legal transmit power limits.
8. Do not use external power amplifiers on any wireless system; their use is illegal and results in disqualification.
9. Bluetooth devices operating in the 2.4 GHz band are permitted for sensors and robot communications only if
they are Class 2 or Class 3 devices (maximum 2.5 mW EIRP).
10. Class 1 Bluetooth devices are not allowed.
11. Zigbee/IEEE 802.15.4 technology operating at 2.4 GHz is prohibited due to interference concerns.
12. Communication systems using other ISM frequency bands (e.g., 900 MHz or 5 GHz) are permitted but will not
be monitored for interference during the competition.
13. Teams using unmonitored frequency bands are solely responsible for managing interference issues; such
issues cannot be used as grounds for protest.
14. Teams may use 5 GHz Wi-Fi for WAP-to-robot communications during competition.
15. Interference on 5 GHz communications will not be monitored and cannot be used as grounds for a protest or
rematch.
16. The communication link must maintain an average data utilization bandwidth of no more than 4,000 Kbps.
17. No peak data utilization bandwidth limit is imposed.

Regulations on Mounting Beacons and Markers:
Teams must follow the guidelines below to ensure fair and normalized runs across all participants.

General Guidelines:
1. Beacons/markers must be placed during the designated setup period (10 minutes).

2. They must be removed promptly after the run during the takedown period (5 minutes).
3. No pre-placed markers or static reference objects may exist outside designated zones.
4. No real-time software updates may be made using external observations while the rover is operating
autonomously or navigating using markers/beacons.
5. Marker-based navigation must operate fully autonomously once the robot is started. This means:
a. No real-time commands based on human observation of markers.
b. No human-in-the-loop interpretation (e.g., reading a QR code and sending commands manually).
c. The robot must independently detect, interpret, and respond to markers using onboard systems such as
computer vision and autonomous logic.
6. All markers/beacons must be:
a. Clearly visible and identifiable.
b. Constructed using non-sharp, non-glare materials.
7. Reflective materials (e.g., mirror tape) are permitted only after approval during inspection.
8. Teams must declare all beacons/markers to the inspection authority and demonstrate safe operation.
9. All wireless equipment must comply with India’s Wireless Planning & Coordination (WPC) regulations.
10. Teams are responsible for placing beacons/markers at valid locations approved by judges. Participants are
advised to bring tripods or poles according to arena dimensions. The organizing team is not responsible for
creating beacon placement infrastructure.
11. Compliance with all beacon and marker placement rules is mandatory. Failure to comply will result in the
rover being disallowed from running.
12. Target beacons may be mounted in the starting area using the provided mounting system or rods pushed into
the sand. Passive fiducials (e.g., AprilTags, ArUco markers) are allowed.
13. Beacons may transmit signals, light beams, or use laser-based detection systems. Only Class I, Class II, or lowpower (<5 mW) lasers are permitted, and eye-safe documentation must be provided during inspection.
14. Beacons total mass counts toward the robot's 80 kg mass limit and they must be self-powered.
15. Beacons or fiducial targets may be attached only to the designated arena frame area for navigation purposes.
The designated area includes any point on the bin frame structure along the perimeter of the starting zone (2
sides). Tape, clamps, or rods pushed into the sand may be used, but screws or fasteners requiring holes are
prohibited. The navigational aid system must be attached during setup time and removed during takedown
time.
